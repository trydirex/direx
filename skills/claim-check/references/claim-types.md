# What counts as a claim, and what counts as support

## Kinds

| Kind | Is | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `price` | Any amount of money, discount or fee | "an audit is only $999", "20% off this month" |
| `promise` | Something the sender commits to do or guarantee | "we refund you in full", "results in 30 days" |
| `number` | Any other count, size, share or date stated as fact | "our 40 auditors", "since 2009" |
| `claim` | Any other statement of fact, about the sender, the recipient or the world | "we cover the whole country", "we understand how shops like yours run" |

One sentence can hold several claims. List each one.

These are not claims, so leave them out: a question, a greeting, an offer to explain or to talk
("I'd be happy to explain how it works"), the sender's own hedge ("we may not be the right fit"),
and a statement about this email itself ("I won't follow up after this").

## Support

A claim is supported when a source says the same thing. Quote the source's words exactly, and name
the source.

- The same meaning in other words counts: "we're four auditors based in Leeds" is supported by
  "We are a team of 4 auditors in Leeds".
- A number, price or date must match exactly. "40 auditors" is not supported by "4 auditors".
- A source that says something close, but not the claim, is not support. Mark the claim `nothing`.
- Your own knowledge is never support. Only the sources you were given count.
- A claim about the recipient is supported only by a source about the recipient, such as their
  own website.

## Needs a person's decision

A `price` or a `promise` with `nothing` behind it needs a person's decision before the text is
sent: either a source that backs it, or a rewrite. Other unsupported claims are marked `nothing`
too, but only prices and promises get the decision mark.
